Mars rover

Sojourner Rover

On 4 July 1997, Mars Pathfinder bounced onto Mars inside a cluster of airbags. Two days later a small six-wheeled rover rolled down a ramp and onto the red soil.

Photo: NASA/JPL — PIA04318

Destination

Mars

Mission

Mars Pathfinder

Mission date

Landed 4 July 1997

Where it remains

Ares Vallis, Chryse Planitia

Chapter 01

Why NASA built it

Mars Pathfinder was part of NASA's Discovery program, designed to show that Mars missions could be faster and cheaper. Sojourner tested whether a small rover could move and do science on another planet.

Chapter 02

How it worked

Sojourner had a mass of about 10.6 kg, six wheels on a rocker-bogie suspension, and a solar panel on top. It carried an Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer (APXS) to measure what rocks and soil were made of, and talked to Earth through the lander.

Chapter 03

What happened during the mission

Designed for about a week, Sojourner operated for roughly 12 weeks. It studied rocks given friendly nicknames like Barnacle Bill, Yogi and Wedge, travelling around 100 metres in total, never far from the lander.

Chapter 04

After the mission

Contact with the lander was lost on 27 September 1997, and with it the link to Sojourner. The lander was renamed the Carl Sagan Memorial Station.

Important discoveries

  • First wheeled vehicle to operate on another planet.
  • Measured the composition of Martian rocks with its APXS instrument.
  • Images of rounded pebbles and the surrounding terrain supported the idea that floods once flowed through Ares Vallis.

Why it matters

Sojourner Rover is more than leftover hardware. It is a time capsule of the engineering, risks and curiosity of its era — and a reminder that the end of a mission is not the end of its story.

Current known status

Last contact with Mars Pathfinder was on 27 September 1997. Sojourner remains near the lander in Ares Vallis; its exact final state is unknown.

Mars Exploration Rover

Spirit Rover

Planned for 90 Martian days, Spirit explored Gusev Crater for more than six years and found evidence of Mars's watery past.

Stuck in soft soil at a site called Troy since 2009. Last communication: 22 March 2010. NASA ended recovery efforts on 25 May 2011.

Apollo 11

Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage

The lower half of the lunar module Eagle. It carried Armstrong and Aldrin to the surface, then became the launch pad they left behind.

Remains at Tranquility Base. The site has been photographed from orbit by NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.

Moon

Apollo 15, 16 & 17

Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle

A battery-powered, foldable car that let astronauts travel many kilometres from their lander. Three were driven on the Moon and all three were left there.

All three rovers remain parked at their landing sites: Hadley–Apennine, Descartes Highlands and Taurus–Littrow.
