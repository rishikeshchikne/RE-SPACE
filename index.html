The silent witnesses

Not everything returns home.

A mission ends when a spacecraft stops sending data or its team stops operating it. But the hardware doesn't vanish. Landers, rovers and instruments stay exactly where they last stood — sometimes for decades, preserved by airless skies or slowly dusted by Martian winds.

Moon

Apollo 11

Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage

The lower half of the lunar module Eagle. It carried Armstrong and Aldrin to the surface, then became the launch pad they left behind.

Remains at Tranquility Base. The site has been photographed from orbit by NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.

Explore story
Moon

Apollo 15, 16 & 17

Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle

A battery-powered, foldable car that let astronauts travel many kilometres from their lander. Three were driven on the Moon and all three were left there.

All three rovers remain parked at their landing sites: Hadley–Apennine, Descartes Highlands and Taurus–Littrow.

Moon

Surveyor 3

Surveyor 3

A robotic lander that tested the lunar soil before humans arrived — and later became the only spacecraft on the Moon visited by astronauts.

Remains in its crater in the Ocean of Storms, minus parts removed by the Apollo 12 crew in 1969.

Mars

Mars Pathfinder

Sojourner Rover

A microwave-oven-sized rover that became the first wheeled vehicle to explore another planet.

Last contact with Mars Pathfinder was on 27 September 1997. Sojourner remains near the lander in Ares Vallis; its exact final state is unknown.

Explore story
Mars

Mars Exploration Rover

Spirit Rover

Planned for 90 Martian days, Spirit explored Gusev Crater for more than six years and found evidence of Mars's watery past.

Stuck in soft soil at a site called Troy since 2009. Last communication: 22 March 2010. NASA ended recovery efforts on 25 May 2011.

Mission legacy

They were built to be left behind.

Every lander that stays, every rover that falls silent, is a record of what we tried and what we learned. Scientists still study these sites, engineers still learn from their designs, and the rest of us can stand at the edge of a crater on another world and know: someone from Earth was here.
