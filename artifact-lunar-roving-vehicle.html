Lunar exploration vehicle

Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle

On the first Apollo landings, astronauts could only explore as far as they could walk. For the last three missions, NASA sent a car folded against the side of the lunar module.

Photo: NASA — AS15-86-11603

Destination

Moon

Mission

Apollo 15, 16 & 17

Mission date

Driven 1971–1972

Where it remains

Hadley–Apennine · Descartes Highlands · Taurus–Littrow

Chapter 01

Why NASA built it

Geologists wanted samples from many places, not just around the lander. A rover could carry two astronauts, tools, cameras and samples across kilometres of terrain in a single moonwalk.

Chapter 02

How it worked

The rover was built by Boeing under NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center. It weighed about 210 kg on Earth, ran on non-rechargeable batteries, and had an electric motor in each of its four wire-mesh wheels. It unfolded from a bay on the lunar module.

Chapter 03

What happened during the missions

On Apollo 15, 16 and 17 the crews drove roughly 28 km, 27 km and 36 km respectively. A television camera on the rover let Mission Control watch, and on the final missions it filmed the ascent stage lifting off.

Chapter 04

After the missions

There was no room to bring the rovers home. Each was parked near its landing site, with its camera pointed so it could film the crew's departure.

Important discoveries

  • Let Apollo 15 crews reach Hadley Rille and collect the "Genesis Rock," a sample of ancient lunar crust.
  • Greatly expanded the area astronauts could sample on each mission.
  • Showed that crewed surface vehicles could work on another world — a lesson for future lunar rovers.

Why it matters

Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle is more than leftover hardware. It is a time capsule of the engineering, risks and curiosity of its era — and a reminder that the end of a mission is not the end of its story.

Current known status

All three rovers remain parked at their landing sites: Hadley–Apennine, Descartes Highlands and Taurus–Littrow.

Surveyor 3

Surveyor 3

A robotic lander that tested the lunar soil before humans arrived — and later became the only spacecraft on the Moon visited by astronauts.

Remains in its crater in the Ocean of Storms, minus parts removed by the Apollo 12 crew in 1969.

Sojourner Rover

A microwave-oven-sized rover that became the first wheeled vehicle to explore another planet.
