Mars rover

Spirit Rover

Spirit and its twin Opportunity landed on opposite sides of Mars in January 2004. Spirit was sent to Gusev Crater, which scientists thought might once have held a lake.

Photo: NASA/JPL/Cornell — PIA05200

Destination

Mars

Mission

Mars Exploration Rover

Mission date

Landed 4 January 2004

Where it remains

Gusev Crater

Chapter 01

Why NASA built it

The Mars Exploration Rover mission's main goal was to search for clues to past water activity on Mars, using a robotic field geologist that could drive to the rocks it wanted to study.

Chapter 02

How it worked

Spirit was a six-wheeled, solar-powered rover with panoramic cameras, spectrometers, a microscopic imager and a rock abrasion tool for grinding into rock surfaces.

Chapter 03

What happened during the mission

The plains near the landing site were volcanic, so Spirit drove to the Columbia Hills — named for the crew of Space Shuttle Columbia. Even after a front wheel stopped working in 2006, it kept going, dragging the wheel and driving about 7.7 km in total.

Chapter 04

After the mission

In 2009, Spirit's wheels broke through a crust into soft sand at Troy. Engineers could not free it. As the Martian winter arrived, it could no longer stay warm and powered, and it fell silent in March 2010.

Important discoveries

  • Found minerals in the Columbia Hills altered by water.
  • Its dragging wheel uncovered nearly pure silica soil, pointing to past hot springs or steam vents.
  • Identified carbonate-rich rock at an outcrop called Comanche, evidence of past wet, non-acidic conditions.

Why it matters

Spirit Rover is more than leftover hardware. It is a time capsule of the engineering, risks and curiosity of its era — and a reminder that the end of a mission is not the end of its story.

Current known status

Stuck in soft soil at a site called Troy since 2009. Last communication: 22 March 2010. NASA ended recovery efforts on 25 May 2011.

Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage

The lower half of the lunar module Eagle. It carried Armstrong and Aldrin to the surface, then became the launch pad they left behind.

Remains at Tranquility Base. The site has been photographed from orbit by NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.

Moon

Apollo 15, 16 & 17

Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle

A battery-powered, foldable car that let astronauts travel many kilometres from their lander. Three were driven on the Moon and all three were left there.

All three rovers remain parked at their landing sites: Hadley–Apennine, Descartes Highlands and Taurus–Littrow.
